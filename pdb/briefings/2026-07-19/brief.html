What we might be getting wrong

  • The Hormuz/crude signal is priced as a fresh tail-risk realization; it is actually the third leg of a five-month-old event, and the modal outcome is retracement, not extension. The strait has been "mostly blocked" since Feb 28; this war has already run the full round trip once (Brent ~$128 Apr 2 → <$70 by Jul 1 after the June MOU, ~$30+ retraced in under 3 weeks). Every comparable premium — Abqaiq 2019 (~2 weeks), Gulf War (~5 months), 2022 (~4 months) — retraced >50% once physical flow resumed. The brief's own falsifier concedes a 55–80% retrace on a re-ceasefire; that makes $88 Brent more likely a local top than a launch pad, and the "well above $90" branch should not be the headline takeaway. Note also the internal inconsistency left standing: Brent w/w stated at ~+14% while an independent source has both benchmarks >+16%, and DXY 100.75 is unverified.
  • The Fed-hike signal's 65% September number is a single-wire snapshot dressed as market fact, sitting on a ~15–20% base rate. One Reuters story (B1) sources both the ~15% July and ~65% September odds; an independent same-day syndication had July at ~12%, and the verifier explicitly says treat these as claimed snapshots. Unconditional history: the Fed has resumed hiking within 9 months of a final cut once in six post-1990 easing cycles (1999). Futures two meetings out have "historically over-signaled action during hawkish-rhetoric clusters," June was 12–0 unanimous, and Jefferson's "reconsider" was conditional, not a hike call — the brief counts him as a third hawk. Two explicit hawks plus a conditional Vice Chair is a thinner coalition than the headline implies.
  • Signals 1 and 2 are treated as reinforcing when they may be offsetting — the consensus narrative misses that oil-shock inflation is historically what central banks look through. The brief's context itself flags that the ECB's Jul 2008 hike into an oil spike, and Apr/Jul 2011, were reversed within ~a year. An $88-Brent demand shock (S&P −1%, semis in a bear market, China at 4.3%) is a growth hit as much as an inflation impulse; a plausible ignored interpretation is that the Hormuz escalation reduces September hike odds rather than validating the hawks. If the ceasefire mechanism fires again, the inflation impulse and the 65% pricing likely (55–80%) deflate together.
  • Kuwait damage extent is 100% Kuwaiti-official self-reporting — no satellite or independent physical assessment exists — and the marginal supply-at-risk is far smaller than "2.4–2.5 mb/d producer" framing implies. Kuwait was already down to ~1.65 mb/d (June) from 2.66 nameplate under force majeure, so most of the export chain the strikes could damage was already throttled. The Abqaiq base rate (5.7 mb/d restored in <3 weeks) says single-barrage damage is repaired faster than first-day reporting suggests; materiality 5 rests on a falsifier (July–Aug KPC loadings) that has not resolved. One cited data point (8 fire teams, one blaze) is garbled — the Kuwaiti statement describes two fires — and the Xinhua source carrying it is graded D2. The "three missiles near Shuwaikh Port" detail remains single-stream.
  • The semiconductor signal's two most vivid numbers are simply wrong, and the sector story may be a valuation unwind wearing an AI-thesis costume. MU −10.6% / AMD −6.9% were July 1 sessions conflated onto July 17 (actual: −0.50%, −1.03%); the AMD ~163x P/E is unverified; the Meta catalyst is 2.5 weeks stale, and Meta's stock rose 6–9% on it — the bearish chip read-through is an interpretation, not an event. Base rate: ~10 of 12 SOX 20%+ drawdowns since 1995 mean-reverted within 6–18 months, and the direct analog (DeepSeek, Jan 2025: −$589B Nvidia day, capex raised within weeks) argues the Moonshot/K3 catalyst is weak evidence of regime change. A −20% move after a +96% four-month run is closer to normal distribution-tail behavior than to 2000. Verified confidence is 0.6 — the lowest in the brief — yet the item still carries "confirmed" status and materiality 8.
  • The European gas signal fails its own arithmetic and argues against its own probability call. The storage gap is ~16pp vs the 5-yr norm (~67.5%), not the headlined 23pp vs "~75%"; the 3,786 GWh/day injection figure is keyed to the superseded 90% target (80% needs ~2,850). The signal's own cited tracker projects ~81% fill by Nov 1 at the current pace, and the EU is 4-for-4 on storage mandates since 2022 — an observed miss rate of zero. The "roughly even chance (45–55%)" of hitting 80% sits below both the tracker projection and the base rate; likely (55–80%) is the defensible band. The consensus miss: 2022 proved high summer prices are self-correcting for storage via demand destruction and cargo reallocation.
  • Status inflation and pseudo-independent sourcing run through the whole brief. All six items are stamped "confirmed" even where the adversarial pass cut confidence to 0.6–0.7 and refuted headline data points; "confirmed event, broken numbers" should not render as a clean confirmed badge. Several "multi-source" confirmations are syndications of one wire (the Fed pricing) or one aggregator (TradingEconomics anchors both the Brent and TTF prints), and the China item's A1 grade attaches to an official print from an interested party with a documented smoothing history — the print is confirmed; the underlying growth rate is not, and the "easing always follows a miss" conditional rests on n=3.
  • The brief is structurally one trade, and its per-item confidences overstate independence. Signals 1, 3, and 6 — and the market-pricing leg of 2 — share a single falsifier: a durable re-ceasefire, the exact mechanism that already fired in June. If it fires again, crude, TTF, the Kuwait materiality case, and the hawkish-Fed pricing all deflate simultaneously; if instead an Abqaiq-class strike lands, they all extend together. The portfolio-level uncertainty is therefore much wider than the 0.6–0.93 item confidences suggest, and the scorecard (Sec. 8) should treat these as one correlated prediction, not five.
